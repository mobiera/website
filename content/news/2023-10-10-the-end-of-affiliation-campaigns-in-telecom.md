The digital advertising landscape has shifted, and telecommunications with it.
One notable change is the decline of affiliation campaigns as a method to
promote digital services. This article looks at the reasons and at the
channels telecom providers can use instead.

## The decline of affiliation campaigns

Affiliation campaigns have long been a staple of the digital marketing
toolkit: partners in affiliate networks promote services in exchange for a
commission on sales or leads. Several factors have reduced their effectiveness.

- **Market saturation.** The telecom market is saturated, and affiliates find
  it harder to generate unique value.
- **Regulation.** Tighter rules on data privacy and advertising standards make
  affiliation campaigns more complex and more exposed.
- **Fraud and non-consensual opt-ins.** Unsustainable growth pushed some
  affiliates to bend consent rules, which led to customer complaints and
  regulatory pushback.
- **Complicated acquisition and diminishing returns.** Consumers grow skeptical
  of promotional content, and the extra consent steps operators added to stop
  non-consensual opt-ins complicated the acquisition journey.
- **Consumer behavior.** A growing preference for authenticity and direct
  engagement from brands, which affiliate marketing often lacks.

## Alternative channels

- **STK-based push marketing.** A technology already on every SIM, repurposed
  as a channel the operator owns: interactive push notifications that work on
  any handset, with no data and no balance. Aircast is Mobiera's SIM-vendor
  agnostic platform for it. Read more: /telecom/aircast
- **Content marketing.** Informative content that establishes expertise and
  builds trust: insights, tutorials, industry news, rather than direct
  promotion.
- **Social media.** A direct line to consumers for service, engagement and
  offers, in a more personal register.
- **Influencer partnerships.** Collaboration with individuals whose audience
  matches the operator's target market.
- **Personalization and data-driven marketing.** Campaigns built on customer
  preferences and behavior, which requires understanding both.
- **Community.** Forums, webinars and events that build belonging and loyalty.

## Conclusion

The decline of affiliation campaigns is an opportunity to repurpose
technologies operators already own and to adopt channels aligned with how
consumers behave now. The future of telecom marketing belongs to the most
relevant voice, not the loudest.
